## Writing style

These rules apply to prose: chat responses, docs, code comments, commit
message subjects and bodies, and PR descriptions. They do not apply to code,
identifiers, command output, log excerpts, or quoted material. Commit
message headers follow the Conventional Commits rules in Git workflow; these
rules govern the wording of the subject and body.

### Content

- State the current fact, not the history of how you got there. Don't leave
  remnants of a wrong turn (a claim you made and then had to correct, a
  rejected approach, "note: earlier this said X but that was wrong") in
  shipped text. If that history is genuinely worth keeping, keep it in the
  conversation or commit history, not in the artifact itself.

### Style

Follow the style principles of Strunk & White's *The Elements of Style*:

- Omit needless words. Cut filler, hedging, throat-clearing ("It's worth
  noting that", "In order to"), and restatement of points already made.
- Prefer the active voice. Use passive when the actor is unknown or
  irrelevant ("the pod was evicted").
- Put statements in positive form: say what is, not what isn't.
- Use definite, specific, concrete language. Name the file, flag, metric,
  version, or error code instead of describing it in general terms.
- Make the paragraph the unit of composition: one topic per paragraph,
  topic sentence first.
- Keep related words together; place the emphatic word or idea at the end
  of the sentence.
- Express coordinate ideas in parallel form, including list items.
- Avoid qualifiers ("very", "rather", "quite", "fairly") and elaborate
  words where plain ones will do.
- Do not overstate. No superlatives, enthusiasm markers, or marketing tone.

Treat these as style guidance, not grammar law. Do not contort sentences to
avoid split infinitives, sentence-final prepositions, or justified passive
voice. When a rule conflicts with clarity or technical precision, clarity
and precision win.

### Punctuation and phrasing

- Never use em dashes and avoid double hyphens; prefer parentheticals,
  colons, and semicolons.
- Use the "this, not that" contrastive format only when it adds real,
  specific value, not as a default rhetorical habit.
